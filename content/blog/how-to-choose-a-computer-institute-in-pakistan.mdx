Choosing a computer institute is a real decision. You will spend your time and money on it, and the wrong choice can mean months with nothing to show. Before you pay any fee, ask these seven questions.

## 1. Will I work on a computer in every class?

Many institutes are mostly lectures. You learn a skill by doing it. Ask whether every student has a computer and works on tasks **during** the class, not just at home. At our institute, every session includes a hands-on lab.

## 2. Can I see the full syllabus and duration?

A good institute can tell you what you will learn **week by week** and how long the course takes. If the answer is vague ("everything", "as long as you need"), you cannot judge what you are paying for. Ask for the syllabus in writing.

## 3. What will I have built at the end?

You should finish with something real: a portfolio, a website, a campaign, an office pack. A final project proves you can do the work, and it is what you show employers or clients.

## 4. Is there an internship or real work experience?

The jump from classroom to real work is where most students struggle. An internship, where you work on real tasks with feedback, closes that gap. Ask how it works: how long, what you do, and whether it is part of the course.

## 5. Is the certificate verifiable?

Anyone can print a certificate. A certificate with a **unique ID that anyone can check online** is much harder to fake and much more useful when you apply for a job. You can check ours at [our verification page](/verify).

## 6. Who teaches, and do they work in the field?

Teachers who actually build websites, design brands or run campaigns teach what is used in real work, not just what is in a textbook. Ask about the teacher's experience, and ask to meet them.

## 7. Are the fees, batches and timings clear?

You should know the total fee, how it can be paid, the batch timing, and the refund terms **before** you pay, in writing. If your family prefers it, ask whether there are **separate batches for men and women** and their timings.

## Red flags

- Promises of guaranteed jobs or very high income.
- Pressure to pay immediately, "today only".
- No syllabus, no teacher introduction, no trial class.
- Only theory and slides, no practical work.

## A quick checklist

Before you join, you should be able to answer yes to most of these:

- I know the syllabus and the duration.
- I will work on a computer in every class.
- I will finish with a real project.
- There is an internship or work experience.
- The certificate can be verified online.
- The fee and timings are in writing.
