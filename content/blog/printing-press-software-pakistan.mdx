A printing press runs on small facts: size, paper, colours, quantity, deadline, rate and advance. In many shops these live in different places. The job card is a carbon slip, the rate is in the owner's head, and the advance is in a notebook next to the till. It works until the same customer returns and nobody remembers what was agreed.

Good **printing press software** brings all of this into one place. Here is what to look for.

## 1. Job cards for every order

Each order should have a digital job card with size, paper, colours, quantity, finishing and delivery date. Everyone in the shop, from the designer to the machine operator, should see the same information, so nothing is lost between the counter and the press.

## 2. Rates and estimates that you do not rebuild each time

Pricing a job from scratch every time is slow and error-prone. The software should store your rates by customer and job type, so you can produce a quote quickly and consistently.

## 3. Customer ledger, advances and balance

For most presses, money in the market is the biggest worry. The software should record every advance and every payment under the customer's name, and show the **current balance** in one search. Records of cash, bank, JazzCash and Easypaisa payments should all be supported.

## 4. Production stages

Jobs move through stages: design, printing, finishing, ready, delivered. A board that shows what is in each stage tells you what is late before the customer calls.

## 5. Paper and ink stock

Running out of a common paper size in the middle of a job costs time and money. Stock tracking, with low-stock alerts, avoids it.

## 6. WhatsApp updates

Customers want to know when their job is ready. Software that sends a WhatsApp message when a job is ready or a payment is received saves your staff from many phone calls, and customers like it.

## 7. Reports you can trust

At the end of the day you should see **how much was collected and how much is outstanding**, without adding numbers by hand. Reports should export to Excel and PDF for your accountant.

## 8. Staff roles and permissions

The owner sees everything. A counter operator sees what they need. A good system lets you control who can change rates, delete records or see profit.

## 9. Easy to use, in plain language

If your staff cannot use it after a short walk-through, it will not be used. Look for a simple screen, minimal typing and support that answers when you call.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- Does it work in a web browser, or do I need special hardware?
- Can I try it, or see a demo with my own kind of jobs?
- Who do I call when something goes wrong, and in which language?
- Can my data be exported if I want to leave?
- Is it built for **Pakistani presses**, with local payment methods and local workflows?
