Freelancing means working for clients on your own terms, from home or anywhere with an internet connection. Many people in Pakistan start freelancing alongside study or a job. The most common question is: **which skill should I learn first?**

We do not promise any particular income. What we can say is how to choose well, and how to learn in a way that leads to real work.

## How to choose a skill

Consider three things:

1. **Demand.** Do businesses regularly pay for this?
2. **Interest.** Will you enjoy practising it for hours every week?
3. **Starting cost.** Can you begin with a normal computer and free or low-cost software?

## Skills that beginners commonly start with

### Graphic design

Logos, social media posts, banners, brand kits. Almost every business needs them, and you can start with a laptop and free or low-cost tools. [Learn what a good course covers](/blog/graphic-designing-course-pakistan).

### Video editing

Reels, YouTube videos, ads and business videos. Short-form video is everywhere, so editors are needed.

### Web development

Websites and web apps for businesses. It takes longer to learn, but the work is varied and clients can be local or international. See the [roadmap](/blog/web-development-roadmap-pakistan).

### Digital marketing

Social media management, ads, search optimisation and reporting. Good for people who like people and numbers. Pairs well with design and writing.

### Content writing and translation

Needs strong English or Urdu. Lower software needs, but you must write well.

## The skills around the skill

Your main skill gets you in the door. These help you keep clients:

- **Clear communication:** reply promptly, confirm the brief, and say when you will deliver.
- **Basic English writing:** for proposals, messages and invoices.
- **Time management:** meet deadlines, even small ones.
- **Using AI tools sensibly:** to draft, research and speed up routine work. They do not replace your judgement.
- **Basic Excel and Google Workspace:** to track work, invoices and clients.

## How to get your first client

1. **Build a small portfolio first.** 5 to 10 pieces of real work. Make up briefs for local businesses if you have to.
2. **Start nearby.** A shop, clinic, school or restaurant around you is an easy first client. Offer a clear, small job.
3. **Use freelance platforms carefully.** Write a specific profile, apply to jobs that fit, and write each proposal for that client.
4. **Ask for a review.** Every happy client is your next reference.
5. **Keep improving.** Your second project should be better than your first.

## Common mistakes

- Learning too many skills at once.
- Underpricing so much that you cannot sustain the work.
- Skipping the portfolio and applying with nothing to show.
- Copying other people's work instead of building your own.
